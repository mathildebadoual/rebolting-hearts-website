About the crag

Gagou is a quartzite crag set in a small canyon cut by a wadi (oued), surrounded by palms, tortoises, and villages with cultivated gardens. The fractured red-ochre walls run 10 to 50 m high, face west, and stay shaded until mid-afternoon. The crag was equipped and documented across eight sectors by CAF Casablanca and Escalade Club de l'Isère, whose topo guide (2017) remains the main reference for the area.

What you'll learn

  • How to inspect existing anchors and bolts for wear and corrosion
  • Hardware types and when to use them
  • Using a drill safely on the rock face, on rappel
  • Route gardening and basic maintenance
  • Bolting ethics: respecting first ascensionists, local access, and style

Why Gagou

Gagou's routes were equipped and topoed back in 2017 — like many Moroccan crags, keeping that hardware safe since then depends on an active local rebolting effort. It's also a practical choice for a first workshop: reachable in a day from Casablanca, with routes spanning easy beginner lines to 6b+, so the group can split between learning to rebolt and climbing the routes once inspected.

Who it's for

This workshop is open to women climbers who want to build real, transferable rebolting skills — whether you plan to help maintain routes in your home area, or just want to understand what keeps the anchors you clip safe. No previous bolting experience is required.
